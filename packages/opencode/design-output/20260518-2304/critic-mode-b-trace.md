# Critic Trace — Mode B (Visual Review)

## Per-Asset Observations

- Asset: `logo-primary.png`
  - What works: Strong central SII-like vertical structure; includes five surrounding node clusters; palette matches deep blue/cyan/purple/green/orange direction; the breakthrough slash gives energy.
  - What doesn't work: Too much glow, beveling, 3D reflection, and circuit detail for a primary institutional mark. It will not scale cleanly. The five outer clusters feel decorative rather than a disciplined sub-brand system. The mark is more sci-fi event emblem than university/research institute identity.
  - Section 5 execution: Uses intelligent grid, five research-front idea, open-source nodes, and glass-linework, but translates them too literally and too decoratively.

- Asset: `logo-horizontal-bilingual.png`
  - What works: Cleanest and most institutionally credible logo asset. The English hierarchy is clear, the white field improves official usability, and the mark is calmer than the primary logo.
  - What doesn't work: The symbol differs materially from `logo-primary.png`, so the system lacks one master mark. Chinese “创 智 学 院” appears over-spaced and less natural as a formal institutional wordmark. Node arcs still feel generic.
  - Section 5 execution: Captures bilingual identity and glass/grid cues, but the research-front and open-source logic is not systematized.

- Asset: `color-palette-research-grid.png`
  - What works: One of the strongest assets. It clearly documents colors, glyphs, bilingual labels, and a five-column research grid. The layout feels like a real brand-system page.
  - What doesn't work: Dense small text risks legibility. Some English microcopy feels generic. It is more convincing as a concept board than as final guidelines, but directionally useful.
  - Section 5 execution: Best translation of Section 5: five fronts, modular grid, AI research motifs, and information-design discipline.

- Asset: `web-hero-research-news.png`
  - What works: Strong website composition, impressive hierarchy, readable hero title, clear card modules, open-source contribution graph motif, and West Bund/glass-campus atmosphere.
  - What doesn't work: The building/skyline composite feels generic and potentially fabricated. Stats such as “600+,” “80+,” and “35+” are unverified. Research-front labels do not match the brief’s five fronts. The interface is close to generic AI dashboard aesthetics and relies heavily on blue glow.
  - Section 5 execution: Good on glass campus, nodes, open-source graph, and high-density collaboration; weak on factual specificity and avoiding generic AI visuals.

- Asset: `admissions-camp-poster.png`
  - What works: High energy, strong recruitment hierarchy, effective 0-to-1 visual metaphor, and good use of orange as admissions/action color.
  - What doesn't work: Feels like a commercial AI bootcamp poster rather than a serious new research institute. Includes fabricated date and invented categories such as “Foundation Models,” “Agentic Intelligence,” and “AI Safety & Alignment” instead of the brief’s five fronts. The 0/1 neon path and skyline are generic AI-event tropes. Very crowded.
  - Section 5 execution: Captures “from 0 to 1,” youth, open-source branches, and West Bund glass, but over-amplifies them into startup-marketing language.

- Asset: `techfest-stage-keyvisual.png`
  - What works: Strong stage-readability; title hierarchy is excellent; the central SII node mark is memorable at distance; branching/converging behavior is clear.
  - What doesn't work: Uses invented categories (“Intelligent Systems,” “Energy Systems,” “Raster Intelligence,” etc.) that do not match the strategy. The neural streams are visually polished but generic. The logo at bottom differs again from other marks.
  - Section 5 execution: Good dynamic behavior and event energy; weak fidelity to the exact five research fronts and distinctiveness beyond AI-conference visuals.

- Asset: `campus-wayfinding-glasswall.png`
  - What works: Best application asset for credibility. The glass-wall context, pylon, floor directory, lab sign, and wall graphics feel plausible. Typography is mostly calm and official. Color is restrained and functional.
  - What doesn't work: Some small signage is hard to read. Wall graphic labels include categories not in the brief, weakening system consistency. The logo/mark relationship still differs from other assets.
  - Section 5 execution: Strongest translation of West Bund glass, modular building grid, spatial system, and institutional application.

- Asset: `opensource-research-card-template.png`
  - What works: Excellent density and modular communication logic; good contribution graph, roadmap, data visualization, and card-template structure; green/purple/orange accents have clear roles.
  - What doesn't work: Contains fabricated project names, model names, URLs, contribution numbers, and labels such as “SII-7B-Instruct.” This is a major credibility issue for a research institute. It still resembles a generic AI dashboard/template board.
  - Section 5 execution: Strong on open-source, forks/merges, contribution graph, and information design; weak on verified institutional content.

## Recommendation Derivation

- Recommendation 1: Build a Consistent, Scalable Master Mark
  - Which observation triggered it? The primary mark is over-rendered and the bilingual logo, website logo, TechFest logo, and wayfinding logo do not behave like one consistent identity.
  - Why this fix and not an alternative? Application refinements will not solve the system if the core mark remains unscalable and inconsistent. A flat master mark is the necessary foundation.
  - How does the suggested imagegen prompt address the issue? It explicitly requests one master symbol, flat vector geometry, no glow/3D, bilingual lockups, monochrome/reverse versions, and small-size tests.

- Recommendation 2: Correct the Research-Front System and Remove Fabricated Content
  - Which observation triggered it? Multiple assets use invented research labels, dates, stats, model names, URLs, and performance claims.
  - Why this fix and not an alternative? The brand’s credibility depends on factual discipline. Simply improving aesthetics would leave institutional risk in the communications system.
  - How does the suggested imagegen prompt address the issue? It locks the five brief-approved bilingual research-front labels and instructs the generator to use placeholder-safe content only.

- Recommendation 3: Rebalance Applications Toward Institutional Credibility
  - Which observation triggered it? The web hero, admissions poster, and TechFest visual are visually polished but too cinematic, glowy, and generic-AI in tone.
  - Why this fix and not an alternative? The brief calls for young/experimental but credible; the best existing proof is the calmer wayfinding asset. The solution is not to remove energy, but to discipline it through layout, typography, and restraint.
  - How does the suggested imagegen prompt address the issue? It asks for a cohesive application suite with restrained glass texture, flat grids, exact labels, no fabricated facts, and explicit avoidance of neural webs, light-trail explosions, robots, and generic blue-gradient visuals.
