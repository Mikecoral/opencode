## High-Level Verdict

**Iterate.**

The system has a credible and coherent foundation. The final SII logo, clean identity board, and institutional applications communicate a serious AI research institute rather than a generic training brand. Young technical audiences and industry viewers are likely to read the work as modern, international, and forward-looking.

The main risk is not lack of quality. The risk is **over-complex event communication**: several posters look impressive but need clearer action information, stronger bilingual hierarchy, and more disciplined spacing in dense lower sections. Public, parent, and government-adjacent audiences may understand the institution as advanced and credible, but may not immediately know what to do next, what the event offers, or why it matters to them.

## Audience Interpretation

### Young AI / Research Talent

They are likely to appreciate the non-traditional academy tone: SII feels modern, research-oriented, and more exciting than a conventional university department. The posters suggest high energy and interdisciplinary ambition. Their main critique would be that some event visuals risk becoming polished but generic AI-future imagery unless each poster carries specific technical hooks, named tracks, or concrete opportunities.

### Industry Partners / Investors

They will see credibility and a forward-looking Shanghai innovation signal. Demo Day and TechFest are the most relevant assets for this segment. Their friction point is business clarity: posters should more explicitly show industry sectors, partner value, project pipeline, application deadline, attendance CTA, and outcome language.

### Government / Education Stakeholders

They will likely read the system as responsible, international, and institutionally safe. The single-color logo and clean board are strong for this segment. The main caution is bilingual discipline: small English / Chinese details must remain accurate, not decorative, and official materials need more controlled typography than AI-generated poster textures.

### Public / Parents / Media

They will likely perceive the brand as advanced and credible, but some assets may feel abstract. Open Lab Week is the most accessible direction. This segment needs human benefit, visit experience, clear explanations, and warmer entry points: who can attend, what they can see, why it matters, and how to participate.

## Priority Fixes

1. **Make posters actionable.** Add date, venue, QR / URL, host line, target audience, and one clear CTA. Every final poster should pass a five-second scan test: what is this, who is it for, when is it, where is it, what should I do?
2. **Tame dense lower sections.** TechFest, Demo Day, and Open Lab Week all benefit from more space around bottom modules, fewer micro-details, and larger labels.
3. **Create segment-specific copy layers.** Keep the same visual system, but swap the supporting copy by audience: technical challenge for young researchers, collaboration / outcomes for industry, credibility / mission for government, experience / access for public audiences. Chinese copy should consistently name the institution as **上海创智学院**.

## Recommendation

Proceed with this visual direction, but do one focused iteration before treating it as a usable campaign system. Prioritize poster templates and production tests over another full logo redesign.
