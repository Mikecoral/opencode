### Category synthesis

- Dominant conventions: blue / teal trust palettes, white UI surfaces, sans-serif wordmarks, course-card grids, partner logos, and credential / outcome messaging.
- AI-learning brands add network graphics, gradients, dark-mode panels, or abstract data/agent imagery to signal future-facing intelligence.
- Nonprofit / public education brands soften the category with green-teal, rounded type, and learner-symbol marks.
- Chinese MOOC portal visual language is more crowded and campaign-driven than global edtech brands; it relies on banner modules and bright promotional graphics.
- What would stand out for 创智学院: a disciplined but expressive visual system that combines Chinese naming power with a modular “intelligence / creation” graphic principle; avoid default blue-only edtech and avoid over-dense portal aesthetics.

### Audience synthesis for visual research

- **Primary learner audience:** doctoral-level AI researchers and direct-PhD candidates, including senior undergraduates, incoming / current graduate students, cross-disciplinary researchers, funded startup founders, and AI engineers eligible for in-service doctoral routes.
- **Secondary decision audiences:** co-building universities, government / education authorities, enterprise partners, industry mentors, venture capital / incubation partners, research collaborators, and media observers of Shanghai’s AI ecosystem.
- **Age / life-stage evidence:** published examples include 2024/2025 doctoral students, a named 25-year-old doctoral student, “00后创业学生,” early-career AI engineers, and young mentors averaging 36. Best-supported range is early 20s to mid-30s, with some older social / in-service candidates possible.
- **Core motivations:** frontier AI access, hard technical problems, autonomy over mentors and topics, high-density peer / mentor network, compute and data resources, rapid iteration, startup / commercialization support, and national / societal impact.
- **Psychographic traits:** high self-drive, risk tolerance, “闯劲,” comfort with ambiguity, researcher-founder ambition, collaborative / peer-learning orientation, and desire to work on “无人区” / potentially world-class breakthroughs.
- **Trust barriers:** institution is new and nontraditional; flexible study paths and failure tolerance can be attractive but may raise questions about rigor, degree recognition, evaluation standards, and career outcomes. Visual identity must therefore pair experimentation with unmistakable academic / institutional authority.
- **What this rules out:** K12 edtech cues, broad AI-literacy bootcamp aesthetics, cartoon robots, over-friendly course-platform UI, and purely corporate upskilling language are poorly aligned with the documented audience.
