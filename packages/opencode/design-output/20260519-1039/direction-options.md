# Direction Options

## Option 1: signal-glass-grid
- Thesis: A white-first AI research identity where mist-silver glass layers and a modular signal grid turn SII into a precise, expandable innovation operating system.
- Fit to brief: Best matches “前沿信号,” five AI research fields, node/module collaboration, admissions/research templates, and the user’s request for white, mist silver, cool gray, glass, and restrained cyan/violet/green accents.
- Risks: Can become generic “AI dashboard” if the grid is decorative rather than tied to SII’s mark, five fields, and real content modules.
- Palette stance: Light-first. Base `#FFFFFF`; mist layer `#F5F7FA`; infrastructure silver `#D7DDE5`; cool gray text `#1E2732` / `#68727F`; signal cyan `#00C8F5` for active paths and CTAs; tiny violet `#746BFF` and proof green `#2EDB85` only for field tags, data states, or motion blips. No large dark backgrounds; near-black only for text.
- Typography stance: Modern bilingual grotesk with clean Chinese black体 and neutral English sans; use a compact monospaced companion only for dates, version IDs, project codes, GitHub/HuggingFace references, and data labels. Titles use sharp 700–800 weight but not condensed to the point of tech cliché.
- Layout/composition: 8px/16px modular rhythm; white and pale-gray cards alternating like Carbon’s light layering model; visible key lines, thin rules, and “signal path” overlays connecting modules. Compositions should feel like glass panels on a research wall: aligned, translucent, and calm.
- Best-fit assets: Variable SII algorithm grid, five-field glyph set, recruiting cards, research result templates, publication cards, website UI, lab signage panels, motion system where nodes activate in sequence.
- Negative cues: No black/cyan cyberpunk hero, no full-rainbow grids, no MIT Media Lab 7×7 imitation, no generic neural-network wallpaper, no traditional university crest frame.

## Option 2: west-bund-glass-editorial
- Thesis: A luminous West Bund research-campus identity that uses white space, architectural glass, and editorial typography to make SII feel high-spec, young, and publicly credible.
- Fit to brief: Strong for Shanghai West Bund / glass curtain wall DNA, global talent recruitment, public-facing lectures, admissions campaigns, and the “国家战略 + 青年实验场” balance.
- Risks: Could become too architectural, luxury real-estate, or magazine-like if the AI signal system is underdeveloped.
- Palette stance: Light-first and nearly monochrome. Base white `#FFFFFF`; warm paper-white `#FAFBFC`; glass silver `#E7EBF0`; shadow gray `#AAB3BE`; ink `#101820`; accent violet `#7A6CFF` as the primary campaign spark; cyan `#00C8F5` only as glints on glass edges or active links; green reserved for proof/success metadata. Avoid red except for legally/strategically unavoidable one-off notices.
- Typography stance: More editorial than UI: large bilingual headlines with confident weight contrast, generous leading, and short phrase breaks; body copy remains highly readable; mono appears as a small annotation layer rather than a dominant tech voice.
- Layout/composition: Asymmetric editorial spreads, large white margins, full-bleed or cropped architectural/people photography, glass-like panels floating over images, vertical bilingual lockups, and calm poster systems for talks and recruitment. Less dense than Option 1; more atmospheric and spatial.
- Best-fit assets: Campus and lab photography art direction, glass/reflection texture library, lecture posters, recruitment pages, admissions campaign key visuals, environmental graphics, welcome walls, bilingual manifesto layouts.
- Negative cues: No dark server-room mood, no glossy real-estate brochure clichés, no heroic stock scientists, no decorative gradients without structure, no red/blue/gold university palette.

## Option 3: proof-network-map
- Thesis: A clean open-source research identity where SII’s collaborations, code releases, papers, ventures, and five AI fields are mapped as proof trails from node to value.
- Fit to brief: Best matches “开源与高速迭代,” mentor/enterprise network, node-to-value symbol route, research outcomes, entrepreneurship, and proof-of-impact storytelling.
- Risks: Can feel too technical or startup-tool-like if human/campus moments are absent; green accent can drift into fintech or sustainability if overused.
- Palette stance: Light-first with proof accents. Base white `#FFFFFF`; lab surface `#F6F8F9`; grid gray `#DDE3EA`; graphite text `#17202A`; proof green `#2EDB85` as the main active accent for validated outputs; cyan `#00C8F5` for signal/input; violet `#746BFF` for model/research categories. Accent coverage should stay below roughly 10–15% per composition.
- Typography stance: Functional research publishing system: clear grotesk for bilingual headings and dense body, with a stronger monospaced layer for repo names, version numbers, benchmarks, methods, and timestamps. Hierarchy leans toward labels, tables, captions, and evidence cards.
- Layout/composition: Network maps, timelines, release notes, matrix cards, taxonomy tags, and diagrammatic arrows from “unknown → prototype → paper/code → venture/public value.” More compact than Option 2; more evidence-led than Option 1.
- Best-fit assets: Node-to-value path mark or secondary graphic, collaboration maps, GitHub/HuggingFace/project cards, benchmark charts, field taxonomy icons, impact reports, incubated venture one-pagers, research-progress dashboards.
- Negative cues: No blockchain/network cliché, no excessive dotted-node backgrounds, no terminal-green-on-black, no cluttered KPI dashboards, no startup growth-hacking aesthetic.

## Recommendation
- Recommended direction: Option 1: `signal-glass-grid`.
- Why this direction should be explored first: It best satisfies the light visual-system constraint while preserving the brief’s AI frontier, modular research-field, collaboration-network, and application-system needs. It can absorb Option 2’s glass/campus atmosphere and Option 3’s proof metadata without becoming either a brochure or a tool UI.
- Which direction to avoid for this brief: Avoid a dark “AI infrastructure/cyber lab” route, even if technically credible; it conflicts with the user’s explicit white-based preference and would likely collapse SII into generic dark tech branding.
